# Entity Models & Animation

> Modular character assembly, NPC/monster models, skeletal animation and combat action playback (Phases 5C, 5D and 5D.1). Status and open work: [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md) and GitHub Issues.

## Models and equipment (Phase 5C)

- Dynamic character mesh decoder stitching Race + Face + 5 Armor Slots (Head, Body, Hands, Legs, Feet) + Weapons from distinct DATs (`CharacterSlot`: Face=0, Head=1, Body=2, Hands=3, Legs=4, Feet=5, Main=6, Sub=7, Ranged=8; playable `CharacterRace`: Hume M/F 1/2, Elvaan M/F 3/4, Taru M/F 5/6, Mithra 7, Galka 8).
- Bind-pose entity rendering at live `WorldEntity` coordinates
- **Gear occlusion (#88).** Worn gear hides the body pieces it covers, as in retail.
  - Every 0x2A mesh declares an `OccludeType` (header byte 3): what it hides on other pieces. Every piece carries a `DisplayType`: byte 13 of the 0x8010 render-properties block in force when the piece is read, 0 before any.
  - `AssembleCharacter` builds the union of occludeTypes across the worn set, and `GearOcclusion` drops each piece it hides:

    | displayType | Piece | Hidden by |
    |---|---|---|
    | 1 | hair | `0x02`-`0x06` |
    | 2, 3 | hair | `0x04`-`0x06` |
    | 4 | face | `0x05` |
    | 5 | wrist | `0x12` |
    | 6 | pants | `0x32` |
    | 7 | shins | `0x22` |

    `0x11`/`0x21`/`0x31` are body/legs/feet self-markers that hide nothing. Models are cached per grap table, so an equipment change reassembles with the new union.
  - A stowed ranged weapon is not drawn (retail scales it to zero until drawn) but its occludeType counts. Drawing it during a ranged attack is #158.
  - Checked on retail Hume Male DATs (`GearOcclusionRealDataTests`, 2026-09-30): full helm head 22 (`0x05`) with sleeved body 3 (`0x12`) hides 14 face-DAT pieces (displayTypes 1, 3, 4) and 2 hand pieces (5), 2374 to 1952 triangles. This matches xi-tools `xi gear pose`.
  - Fixed NPC models get the same rule (#181): `LoadMonsterModel` takes the union over the model DAT's own meshes. Humanoid NPCs such as Apururu (model 164: hat `0x04`, face mesh with hair displayTypes 1 and 3; 2246 to 1848 triangles) bundle gear meshes in one DAT. A scan of models 1-3999 (2026-10-01) found 300+ models with pieces to hide, all humanoid NPCs; monster DATs hide nothing.
  - Rule and byte positions referenced from xi-tools `docs/gear/pose.md` ("Hidden pieces", after xim `ActorModel.isOccluded`).
- **Where a PC's look comes from.** Other players: the grap id table in S2C `0x00D` (only when the Model send flag is set). Your own character: S2C `0x00A` at zone-in, then S2C `0x051` `GRAP_LIST`, which LSB sends at login, game-ok, on every visible equipment change and on lockstyle. `SessionNetworkManager` keeps the latest table and reapplies it to the local entity. `ResourceManager.TryLoadEntityModel` caches one assembled `EntityModel` per race, face and full grap table. `EntityRenderer` caches GPU buffers per `EntityModel` instance, not by model name: a PC model is named `{race}_Face{n}` whatever it wears, so a name key made same-race, same-face characters share the first one's gear (#153). When `ResourceManager.ClearCache` runs it bumps `CacheGeneration`, and `EntityRenderer` then frees its GPU copies of the dropped models.
- NPC, Monster, and Trust model rendering from DAT resource caches (monsters/NPC models resolve via `EntityModelOffset = 98239` to `98239 + modelId`).
- NPC-only child races (look race 29 Mithra kitten, 30 girl, 31 boy; 115 server NPCs such as Southern San d'Oria's Authere and Blendare): skeletons `ROM/61/110`, `ROM/61/58`, `ROM/61/85` carry their own idle / walk / run clips, and each outfit slot resolves to `slotBase + modelId` (0-19 Hume, 20+ Elvaan variants).
- [ ] **Actor status visuals:** effects attached to characters by status effects (e.g. the Refresh/Regen afterglow on the characters in the Bibiki Bay captures). Can reuse the actor effect runtime below.
- **Model-embedded effects (#9):** a model DAT's Section 0x05 generators run on its actor. `ActorEffectLoader` parses the DAT with the zone effect parser in actor mode: generators of every attach type except sun/moon, all as emitters, plus the DAT's Section 0x07 routines by name. `ResourceManager.GetActorEffects(modelId)` caches the result per model and skips DATs without generators. `ZoneTerrainRenderer` runs one `ActorEffectInstance` per spawned NPC or monster with effects, simulated in the actor's model space (raw DAT axes, camera taken into that space). The instance's auto-running generators emit from spawn, and `Play(routine)` starts the others. Particles are drawn in the zone effect pass, placed by the actor's model transform from `EntityRenderer.ActorAnchors`, at the skeleton joint reference the generator names: attach bits 4-9 for source-side types, bits 10-15 for target-side ones (xi-model-viewer `getActorAttachPosition`).
  - Effect-only models now draw. The Home Point crystal (model 51, `ROM/3/25.DAT`) shows its eight idle layers (crystal `bnd0`, aura shells, cross planes, ground rings, sparkle cards); the skeleton mesh is a 3 mm placeholder (xi-tools `docs/dats/ROM_3_25.md`). Verified offscreen in Southern San d'Oria (2026-09-28).
  - Scope from a scan of model ids 1-3192 (file id = model + 1300 / + 50295 / + 96907): 648 models carry drawable generators, 379 of them auto-running (idle) ones, e.g. elementals 8-15, Home Points 51/55/57, and portals.
  - [ ] Effect routines are not played from the network yet: S2C `0x038` (`bind` on Home Point use, sent by a LandSandBoat module) and the particles of `0x028` action routines (their motion does play, see Phase 5D.1). (A cross-zone Home Point warp currently leaves the player stuck in an event: #122.) Target-side generators (the `bind` flash on the player's joints) ride the effect's own actor until actions bind a target.
  - [ ] Particles always move with their actor; generators that leave particles behind (`FollowGenerator` off) and the follow-actor updater `0x11` are not modelled yet. The mapping from actor facing to particle axes is only checked on the rotationally symmetric Home Point.
  - [ ] Actor-attached weather effects (`weat/clod/tobi` birds in zone DATs) are a separate mechanism: zone weather generators attached as target-actor/source-facing that draw a weighted mesh (`0x1D`/`0x25`, not decoded yet): #121.

## Skeletal animation (Phase 5D)

- FFXI bone hierarchy & joint matrix tree parser (Section `0x2B` `SkeletonAnimationDecoder`, GPU joint-palette skinning in `EntityRenderer`/`ZoneShaders`)
- Quaternion normalized-lerp (NLERP, matching documented retail behavior) rotation & translation keyframe interpolation (`AnimationClip.TrySample`, `SkeletonPoseEvaluator.EvaluatePose`)
- Full-body locomotion & battle motion packs enabled by default (`EnableSpeculativeMotionPacks = true`). Resolved origin blob collapse root cause (additive translation kinematics $t = t_{\text{bind}} + \Delta t$, Hamilton rotation composition, normalized multi-rate track sampling phase alignment, and folder-carry legacy battle pack file number resolution). Resting locomotion is preserved against battle locomotion clobbering, and in-game renderer supports battle stance (`"btl"`) and death (`"ded"`).
- Authentic entity animation state & motion resolution: monster suffix resolution (`idl0`, `wlk0`, `run0`), packet `MovTime` parsing ensuring stationary NPCs idle instead of running in place, and zero-jitter camera-synchronized player mesh positioning with boundary-accurate keyframe interval interpolation.
- Generalized NPC stance & dual-channel animation blending (`NpcStanceResolver`, `SkeletonPoseEvaluator.EvaluateBlendedPose`, `EntityAnimationState`): convention-based auto-discovery across multi-stance mob families (Omega quadruped/biped, Uragnite shell, Hpemde open/submerged, Adamantoise guard), one-shot transition sequencing (`sp00`/`sp10`/`sp20`/`sp30`), strict priority interruption (`Death` > locomotion > stance transition), and zero-distortion local-space NLERP pose blending.

## Transient combat and action animation (Phase 5D.1)

S2C `0x028` actions play on their actor through the model's own **motion routines** (Section 0x07), then blend back to the stance; each result shows on its target when the actor's routine reaches its hit.

- **Motion routines (`MotionRoutineDecoder`, `EntityModel.MotionRoutines`).** Every model and motion pack carries named 0x07 routines: the swings `ati0`-`ati2` (standing) and `atf0` / `atb0` / `atl0` / `atr0` (moving), the counters `cni0` / `cnf0`..., the chants `cabk` / `cawh` / `cast`..., the releases `shbk` / `ssbk` / `shot`..., the item use `cait` / `shit`, the draw `out0` and sheathe `in 0`, the reactions `damg` / `gurd` / `pary` / `gur1`, death `dead`. The decoder reads op `0x05` (play clip: reference with a `?` body-region wildcard, `+0x10` f32 speed, `+0x18` u16 blend-in ticks, `+0x1C` blend-out, `+0x1E` loop count), the links `0x03` / `0x3B` / `0x57` / `0x3C` (followed into the same model; `0x3B` / `0x3C` wait for the child), a link to `dada` / `mdam` as the hit tick, op `0x21` / `0x25` flinch (`+0x18` f32 ticks) and op `0x5A` pose flash (`+0x0E` u16 pose, `+0x14` f32 ticks). Routines run at 60 Hz; delays are the wait after a command. A PC's routines come from its race base (`ROM/27/82` for Hume male) with the weapon's battle pack (`ROM/32/13`... per `CharacterEquipmentResolver`) overriding. Field layout read from the retail DATs (2026-09-28), with the op table from xi-tools `docs/fx/effect_system.md` and `docs/ability/mixer.md`.
  - Measured on Hume male: the swings play `at0?` / `at1?` / `at2?` and land at tick 34-40 of 88-118; each weapon pack maps its routines to its own clips (`ROM/32/15`'s `ati2` plays `at3?`), so clips are never hardcoded. The chants loop their clip 8-63 times (treated as sustained until replaced). Monster 1600: swings land at ticks 22-50; `cawh` / `cate` link `cast` (`ma0?` x10); `shwh` links `shot` (`ma1?` then `ma2?`); `gurd` / `pary` play the two-tick `gud?` pose with a 16-tick blend-out.
- **Dispatch (`ActionPlaybackQueue`, #10).** Subscribes to `CombatState.ActionExecuted` and posts `ActionRequest`s to the actor's `EntityAnimationState` (thread-safe queue, played on the render thread). By `cmd_no`: a basic attack posts one swing per result (a double attack or dual-wield round swings each hit in turn); the start packets (magic, item, ranged, readied skill) carry the actor's routine FourCC in `cmd_arg` (`cabk`, `cait`, `calg`, `cate`), or an `sp??` one when interrupted (the chant stops); a finished spell plays the release of the last chant (`ca??` to `sh??`, else `shot` / `shbk`); a finished item plays `shit`; job abilities play the `cm0` clip. Semantics from XiPackets `world/server/0x0028`.
  - Hits: the actor's state delivers a request's hits at its routine's hit tick (a routine without one, e.g. a release, at its second clip step or tick 36). When the actor is not animated the queue delivers them itself (`Update`, once per frame from the viewport): 0.7 s after arrival if no state took the request, 3 s if it waits in a queue, hit tick + 0.5 s once started. Requests that waited over 2 s are skipped with their hits shown; at most 3 wait behind the playing one.
- **Swing resolution (#11).** The weapon type picks the battle pack (main weapon DAT Info byte 3), whose routines are the swings. Standing: one of the pack's `ati*` at random (every pack has three; retail shows no fixed order, and the result's `sub_kind` right / left / kick has no routine of its own in the packs checked). Moving: the routine for the direction (`atf0` forward, `atb0` back, `atl0` / `atr0` strafe), which may keep playing while moving; with none, nothing plays and the hit still lands. Models without swing routines fall back to their `at0`-`at2` clips.
  - [ ] **Open: hand-to-hand hands and kicks, dual-wield off hand.** With hand-to-hand, a round's results alternate right (`sub_kind` 0) and left (1) hand, and kicks (2 / 3) come as extra hits with their own formulas. The unarmed pack `ROM/32/13` (Hume male) and the alternate hand-to-hand / katana pack `ROM/98/55` have only `ati0`-`ati2` (`at0`-`at2`) and no kick clip. Measured peak hand travel from the battle stance (2026-09-29): all three land with the right hand (about 1.0-1.1 yalms at the 0.67-0.73 s hit); only `at1` also moves the left hand early (0.76 yalm at 0.3 s, a jab before the right). No foot lifts in any of them. The weapon packs have no off-hand routine for dual wield either. So the left hand and kicks are probably made another way (a mirrored clip, or the basic-attack routine `atk0`, whose undecoded ops `0x24` / `0x32` may pick the swing from `sub_kind`). Until checked, every swing is a random standing variant. To check in game with Windower: what a left-hand hit and a kick look like in a hand-to-hand round, and an off-hand hit with dual wield.
- **One-shot playback and stance recovery (#12).** The action owns the body: each clip step blends in over its blend-in ticks, plays once and holds (or loops for a chant), and at the routine's end the body blends back to the stance clip over the last step's blend-out (the finished clip holds its last frame while fading: `PreviousClipLoops`). Death and a model change end it at once; moving ends a standing action; stopping ends a moving swing once its hit has landed. Engaging and disengaging while standing play the weapon draw `out0` / sheathe `in 0`; the weapon moves between rest and hands halfway through (`WeaponGripOverride`; halfway is an approximation of the routine's op `0x1F` at tick 36 of 72).
  - Weapon skills, monster TP moves and pet skills play a stand-in swing: their bodies live in separate animation DATs (the weapon-skill banks of xi-tools `docs/anim/weapon-skills.md`), not played yet (#132).
- **Hit reactions (#13).** A hit with distortion (`scale & 3`: 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1) flinches the target toward the damage pose `dfm` (attacker in front) or `dbm` (behind) over the model's `damg` flinch length (24 ticks), rising over the first quarter and easing back. The flinch is additive: each joint turns by the pose's offset from its neutral twin (`dfi` / `dbi`, `dfi6` / `dbi6` on the PC motion packs) on top of whatever is playing. Measured: `dfm` and `dfi` differ on 4-11 joints of the monsters checked while both sit 25-35 degrees off the stance clips, so an absolute blend would drag the whole body into another stance. Guard / parry / block play their routine as a pose flash (PC op `0x5A`: pose 0 `gdm`, 1 `pym`, 16 ticks; monsters their `gud?` clip, absolute); poses without a twin blend absolutely, masked to the joints they really drive (the loader grafts idle tracks into PC battle-pack poses). A counter reaction makes the target play its counter swing. Misses and spikes show nothing on the body. Checked by CPU-skinning Hume male and monster 1600 poses offscreen (2026-09-29); in-game check pending.
- **Engagement end (#67).** The local player's engagement ends (no disengage request is sent; the server has already ended it) and the fought target is dropped when S2C `0x037` status leaves 1 (engaged), or the engaged target dies (`Hpp` 0 or status 2 / 3) or despawns; the stance change then plays the sheathe. A selected target that despawns is dropped too. Status values from LandSandBoat `data/enums/animation.yaml`.
- **Event poses (#86).** A running event (cutscene) can own an entity's place: `WorldEntity.EventPose` (position, heading, walk speed) replaces the server position and heading while set, drawn on the floor below it (`EntityGrounding.GetDisplayHeight(Vector3, ...)`); the walk speed picks the walk (up to 2.5 yalms/s) or run clip, else idle. `IsEventHidden` keeps the entity from being drawn. The local player's controller position is left alone (it is what the client reports). Both are set and cleared by `EventDialogController`; see [stock-ui.md](../ui/stock-ui.md) (Cutscene staging).
- **Body-region clip parts (#163).** Fixed NPC models (a monster-style model id rather than a race and gear look: Curilla model 69, Prince Trion model 64, 179 of the 1,222 models with clips in ids 1-1499) store each motion as body-region parts, e.g. `wlk0` (16 joints: legs), `wlk1` (73: upper body, arms and the weapon joints), `wlk2` (10: waist). The stem name (`wlk`) used to point at the first part in file order, so a walk moved the legs alone with the upper body in its bind pose and the sword at the floor, `run` on Curilla was the waist part, and a model whose first part is the waist slid without a walk cycle. `EntityModelLoader.MergeBodyRegionParts` now joins the parts of a stem into one clip when they animate disjoint joints (every joined stem in the survey is such a set: `idl`, `wlk`, `run`, `btl`, `at00`-`at02` and so on); same-stem clips that move the same joints stay separate motions. Player looks were not affected: their packs are merged by `MergeLocomotionCategories`. Event motion banks store their gestures the same way (Curilla's bank 140, file 32244: `tlk0` legs, `tlk1` upper body with the sword joints 84 / 96, `tlk2` waist), and `EventMotionBank` joins them with the same rule (`EntityModelLoader.JoinBodyRegionParts`); with the legs part alone her arms and sword fell to the bind pose while she talked.
- **Fixed NPC weapons and textures (#163, second round).** A fixed model's `init` routine sets its weapons' starting visibility with op 0x75 (`HideWepControl`: u32 hide, u16 slot; the same op `ROM/0/0.DAT`'s `hwmg` uses to stow main and sub for a cast): Prince Trion's model 64 runs `wof4` from `init`, hiding slot 4, the sword and scabbard of its `wep4` folder (`won4` shows them). The loader leaves out the `wepN` meshes of the slots `init` hides (`EntityModelLoader.InitialHiddenWeaponSlots`); drawn anyway they hung at his hips pointing outward. A script or motion showing the weapon later (`won4`) is not played yet. Curilla's model 69 has no `init` and keeps her sheathed sword. Textures are cached on the GPU by where they came from, never by name (see *Resource cache keys* in [rendering/viewport-and-terrain.md](../rendering/viewport-and-terrain.md)): 1,009 texture names in fixed models 1-1499 carry different pixels in different models, and the name key drew another model's face texture on Balasiel (model 139) once both were loaded.
- **Event gestures (#165).** An event's 0x2C / 0x5B / 0x66 post an `ActionMotion.EventMotion` request: it ends the action playing at once and plays the named routine from the event motion banks loaded onto the entity (`EntityAnimationState.AddEventMotionBank`, `EventMotionBank`: a `mot_` DAT's 0x07 routines and their 0x2B clips, named with the models' three-letter wildcard, e.g. `tl1?`), newest first, else the model's own; its clips come from that bank. `ActionMotion.EventMotionStop` (0x50) ends it by name. `GetRoutineFrames` tells the event how long a routine plays (a looping last clip once), from the banks or the model last drawn. The banks are authored for the skeletons the scripts give them to. Details in [ui/stock-ui.md](../ui/stock-ui.md) (*Cutscene schedulers*).
- **Event pose smoothing (#165).** The event VM moves a pose once per game tick (16-31 ms, irregular), which drawn as is made cutscene walks stutter and turns snap. `EventPoseSmoother` (render thread, per entity) follows a walking pose at its walk speed (up to 1.25 times, to catch up), eases turns toward the new heading (exponential, 8 per second: about 95% in 0.4 s; retail's turn rate is not measured) and takes a placement (a move while standing, or a jump farther than half a second of walking, at least 1.5 yalms) at once. When the event ends, an entity it placed snaps back to its server position and heading. An event walk (0x1F) moves its height in a line toward the goal's (the goal height is often not the floor's: the Southern San d'Oria knights walk from height -2 to a goal at 0 over a floor at -2 that steps down to 0 near the end; taking the goal height at once drew them 2 yalms under the floor, where no floor lies below), and an event pose is drawn on the floor up to `EntityGrounding.EventStepUpHeight` (2.5 yalms) above it.
- **Emotes (#176).** The player races' emotes (`/clap`, `/bow`...) live in six motion DATs per race from its emote file number on (Hume male 32040 = ROM/32/40, Hume female 37013, Elvaan male 41114, Elvaan female 46075, Tarutaru male 51037, female 51071, Mithra 56041, Galka 61008; file numbers from xi-tools `_EMOTE_MOTION_FILE`, converted with `MotFileNoToPath`). Each holds eight routines `em00`-`em07` playing one clip as body parts 0 and 1 (`clp0` legs, `clp1` upper body); part 2, the waist, is in the file six numbers on (`ROM/32/46` for Hume male; a robe body is said to use +12, not done). Emote slot n is file n / 8, routine n % 8. The slots' clips (read from the Hume male DATs, 2026-10-01): 0 `bow`, 1 `poi`, 2-4 the salutes `sl1`-`sl3`, 5 `kne`, 6 `lau`, 7 `wee`, 8 `den`, 9 `nod`, 10-11 `wav`, 12 `wel`, 13 `gla`, 14 `che`, 15-16 `clp`, 18-19 `pok`, 20 `sta`, 21 `sig`, 22 `cmf`, 23 `sur`, 24 `why`, 26 `blu`, 27 `ang`, 28 `ups`, 31 `pan`, 34 `thk`, 35 `fum`, 38 `gut`, 39 `rx0`/`rs0`/`rx2`, 40 `def`; the others play nothing (face-only emotes such as /smile, slot 17). Against the emote ids (C2S 0x05D, LandSandBoat `Emote`: 0 point, 1 bow, 2 salute, 3 kneel ... 13 clap, 14 praise) this is slot = id + 2 from kneel on, with point and bow swapped and salute taking one of three slots (`EmoteMotion.Slot`; the variant byte is presumed to choose, not verified). `EmoteMotion.LoadBank` gives one emote as an `EventMotionBank` with a single routine `emote<id>` (the files' own `em0n` names repeat) and the clips of both files, which the bank joins by stem as for the event gestures. Only the event opcode 0x6E plays them so far; the S2C 0x05A emote echo is [#110](https://github.com/jimmy58663/GordianXI/issues/110).
- **Event head look (#174).** An event's look (`WorldEntity.EventLook`, opcodes 0x1E / 0x4A / 0x79) turns the entity's head toward the entity it looks at while its body keeps its heading. The head is the joint of skeleton reference 5 (`HeadLook.HeadJoint`): on every race skeleton it is the joint the face mesh is bound to (Hume male 52, Hume female 30, Elvaan male 30, Elvaan female 57, Tarutaru 7, Mithra and Galka 40, checked against the face DATs' vertex weights, 2026-10-01), and the fixed NPC models checked (Curilla 69, Trion 64) carry the same references (57, 30); reference 3 is the neck and reference 2 the overhead point. After the pose is evaluated, `HeadLook.Apply` turns the head joint and every joint below it (face, hair, helmet) about the head joint in model space: a turn by yaw is RotY(+yaw) there, which equals turning the body by yaw through the renderer's flip and heading rotation (`HeadLookTests`). The turn is the bearing to the target less the body's heading, clamped to 60 degrees and eased at 8 per second (neither measured against retail); without a look the head eases back. The head also tilts toward the target's head (`HeadLook.TargetPitch`): the angle from its own head joint to the target's, both read from the last drawn pose, clamped to 45 degrees and eased at the same rate; in model space (facing +X, Y down) a tilt up is RotZ(-pitch) taken after the turn. The maintainer's retail recording of the Windurst Waters intro as a Galka (2026-10-02, about 2:04) shows Ajido-Marujido tilting his head well back to look up at the player from about a yalm and a half away, and Apururu doing the same; the limit and rate are not measured.
- **Fixed look axis (#188, provisional).** 0x79 sub 2 (`79 02 actor:u32 x:work y:work`, XiEvents OpCodes/0x0079) sets look mode 2 and two values, retail `LookAxisX` / `LookAxisY`, on an actor whose model is ready (`Render.Flags0` bit 0x200, the same check as 0x23 and 0x2C). Here it sets `EventLook.Fixed(x, y)` and 0x7B or the next look ends it. 0x59 sub 2 / 3 set the head turn speed (retail `TurnSpeedHead`: sub 2 the event's own entity from the work value at +2, sub 3 the actor at +2 from +6), kept in `WorldEntity.EventHeadTurnSpeed`. The renderer reads the axis as a turn (x) and a tilt (y, positive = up) of 4096 steps to a turn, clamped like a look at a target (`HeadLook.AxisAngles`), and moves the head toward it at a constant rate of the speed in steps per second, 50 when the event set none (`HeadLook.AxisStep`, `DefaultAxisTurnSpeed`).
  - *Evidence.* A scan of the retail event DATs of zones 0-299 (2026-10-02) finds 143 script entries with 0x79 sub 2, mostly immediate pairs: 0, ±256, ±512, ±768, ±1000 / ±1024, a few others (127 / 159, 400 / 600, 300 / 420, -10 / -1000). 23 of them set a head turn speed first: 20 to 1500, mostly 100 to 250 (sub 3 200 32 times, 250 20 times, 100 19 times). Port Jeuno event 324 (Abyssea "A Journey Begins", LandSandBoat `A_Journey_Begins.lua`, on zoning in) holds the player on (0, 1024) for 121 frames and sets no speed. The maintainer's retail recording of it (2026-10-02) puts that hold on the shot from behind the player that ends as the pink flash appears in the sky above them (about 1:11-1:13, the 0x7B on the cut). The back of the head stays square to the camera (no sideways turn) and the hat shifts only a few pixels in the 2 s. The next look, 0x4A at the flash's invisible marker 0x010F608F, shows the face clearly tilted up in the front shot after it. Port Jeuno 334 and 341 set (1024, 0) and (768, 0) on Joachim during his lines, the second on "why did the sky take on a bloody red?".
  - *Not known.* Which value turns and which tilts, and their signs: y as the tilt follows the player looking up at the sky in 324 and the missing sideways turn. Also the unit (4096 to a turn, or a normalised ±1000), the speed unit and default (50 steps per second fits the few degrees retail shows in 2 s), and how far retail lets the head go. A retail recording of Joachim in Port Jeuno 334 or 341 from the front would settle the reading.
- **Talking mouth and blink (#185).** Every humanoid skeleton carries two face clips beside its body motions: `mou4` turns the mouth joint (one joint under the head: Hume male 54, Hume female 32, Elvaan male 34, Elvaan female 63, Tarutaru 11, Mithra 46, Galka 44) open and shut over 31 frames at 15 fps (2 s), and `eye3` closes the eyelid joints and opens them again (Elvaan male 33 and 42, Tarutaru 14-17; 4-11 frames, 0.13-0.33 s). Checked on the eight race skeletons and the fixed NPC models 32, 163 and 164 (2026-10-01, `FaceMotionTests`); monster skeletons have neither. The face mesh's lip and eyelid vertices blend between the head joint and these joints (Elvaan male face 2: lips on 30/34, eyelids on 30/33). The face texture's corner patches are not alternate frames swapped in: the dark square is the inside of the mouth and the other patch the eyeball, both always mapped, and the joints move the skin over them.
  - `FaceMotion` layers the clips on the evaluated pose before the head look: each tracked joint turns by the clip's local rotation on top of the body motion (model-space turn Rparent · D · Rparent⁻¹ about the joint, parents first), so on the rest pose it equals playing the clip. The mouth plays `mou4` once (three flaps over 2 s) each time the entity speaks an event line (`WorldEntity.SpokenLines`, counted by `EventDialogController.PrintMessage` for the line's speaker), and a new line restarts it. The first build looped it for as long as the entity's look had a speech frame (0x1E / 0x4A / 0x79 until 0x7B); in the maintainer's in-game test (2026-10-02) the mouths never stopped, and Ajido-Marujido kept flapping through Apururu's lines, where retail moves the mouth a few times as the line is spoken and then stops. A blink plays every 2-6 s (uniform random, not measured against retail), never during the death motion, which closes the eyes itself. - **Knockback (#14).** Knockback is not a server placement: it is the upper 3 bits of each target result's `scale` field in S2C `0x028` (shared with hit distortion), and the client slides the character itself, then reports the new position via `0x015` (LandSandBoat trusts it). When a hit with a knockback level lands on the local player (`ActionPlaybackQueue.HitLanded`, wired in `CharacterSession`), `PlayerLocomotionController.ApplyKnockback` pushes it away from the attacker through `MoveHorizontally`, so walls (`ZoneCollisionMesh.ResolveWalls`), ledges and ground following apply as for walking; a server placement cancels the slide. Remote entities follow their server positions as usual.
  - Levels 1-7 (`KnockbackSettings`): push per tick / damper / timer from LSB `enums/action/knockback.h` (Level 1 = 0.083, 0.075, 5; Level 7 = 0.167, 0.05, 45; the same values as XiPackets' client table). How the client integrates them is undocumented; GordianXI moves the push each 60 Hz tick, shrinking it by the damper fraction per tick, for the timer's ticks: about 0.36 yalm at level 1, 1.05 at level 4, 3.0 at level 7. Calibration against a retail capture pending.
  - **Anchor toggle (built-in Windower "Anchor"):** `/anchor [on|off]` ignores knockback; off by default for legacy parity.
  - **Server kill-switch:** `FeatureRestrictions.KnockbackOverride` (bit 8) keeps knockback on whatever `/anchor` says, following the collision-toggle pattern (`CollisionSettings`).
  - Out of scope by design: discarding WPOS (`0x05B`/`0x065`) placements. Ignoring them would defeat monster draw-in, GM/script teleports and event positions, so GordianXI always honors them and offers no toggle.
