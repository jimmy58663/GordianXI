# Terrain, Actor & Point Lighting

> How Section 0x2F environment colours, point lights and sub-environments light terrain and actors. Calibrations were made against Windower captures of the legacy client. Status and open work: [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md) and GitHub Issues.

## Terrain lighting from 0x2F (chunk 1)

- **Chunk 1: Sand & Terrain Lighting Balance:** Calibrated diffuse and ambient lighting coefficients (`0.5 * amb + 0.5 * df0`) in terrain shaders to prevent overexposure of light-colored ground/sand while maintaining shadow depth. *Superseded (2026-09-24, legacy parity):* terrain/entity lighting is now `clamp(amb + sun + moon)` with 0x2F colors converted as the legacy client does (ambient `/510` + dark bias, clamp 0.5; sun/moon `* diffuseMult` + dark bias), and a moonlight term opposite the sun. *Superseded again (2026-09-26):* the 0x2F bytes now feed the lights as is, ambient = byte/255 and sun/moon = byte/255 x `light_power`, with no dark lift, no halving and no clamp before the lit vertex result (`ZoneEnvironmentSettings.AmbientToLight`/`DiffuseToLight`). Calibrated on a Windower capture of Bibiki Bay at 15:58 (Windower (642.7, 879.3, -20), `suny`): flat sunlit sand measured (182,105,77) in retail against (137,93,66) from the halved ambient; the byte/255 conversion renders it at (184,103,74) and brings the east-facing cliffs from (60,43,40) to (73,52,47) against retail's (76,50,47). Shadows were ~40% too dark before, which read as an over-strong sun. Particle daylight tints clamp at 1 (`StrongestLight`). Noon: see below (#7); retail clips there too, and GordianXI tones it down on purpose.

## Terrain highlights at noon (#7)

- **Retail clips to white.** The maintainer's capture `Retail Captures/Bright_Sand.png` (Valkurm Dunes, zone 103, about 13:17, clear, 2026-10-17) shows the flat sunlit sand saturated to pure white across the lower frame; only shaded dips and the cliffs keep texture. Our unclamped light gives the same: the summed light hits its clamp (1) and the sand texel is about 0.75, so 2 x light x texel is about 1.5 before the framebuffer clips it (measured offscreen at display (-150, ground, 0), 13:17, `fine`).
- **Deliberate exception to legacy parity.** The maintainer: "We don't have to match this, I would rather it be toned down to be more tolerable to look at." Terrain (opaque, cut-out, blended and water submeshes; not actors, effects or sky) now rolls off its highlights: a lit colour channel above `TerrainHighlightKnee` (0.65) keeps knee + e / (1 + e / (ceiling - knee)) of its excess e, approaching `TerrainHighlightCeiling` (0.9) instead of clipping (`ZoneShaders` `ToneTerrain`, uniform `ZoneSceneUniform.TerrainTone`). Valkurm's noon sand draws at about (215, 215, 212) instead of (255, 255, 255).
- **Why on the colour, not the summed light.** Bibiki Bay's calibrated 15:58 sand (above) already sits at the light clamp in red and green, so a shoulder on the light would have darkened it. On the output colour it only touches channels above 0.65: the 15:58 sand's red (0.72) moves by one level (168 to 167 mean over the sand), the 19:50 dusk cliffs (under 0.6) not at all.
- **Retail brightness (opt-in).** `ViewportSettings.RetailTerrainBrightness` (settings file; no Settings UI yet) restores the plain clamp: `ViewportRenderSettings.RetailTerrainBrightness` sets `ZoneTerrainRenderer.RetailTerrainBrightness` each frame, which zeroes `TerrainTone`.
- Open on #7: the actor light cap (0.75) and the sun glow behind `clod` / `suny` are still unchecked against retail.

## Per-vertex terrain lighting (#7 round 2, F10)

- Zone meshes are lit per vertex (Gouraud), as the legacy client's fixed-function pipeline does (`TerrainShaders`): the vertex shader sums ambient + sun + moon (opposite the sun) + the placement's point lights, times the vertex colour, clamps to 1 at the vertex, and the fragment shader interpolates that before the modulate-2x, the highlight shoulder and fog. Actors keep their own shaders.
- Why: the maintainer's capture outside Selbina (`image-13.png`, Valkurm Dunes, TESTING-2026-10-18 round 2) shows a soft grey band on the sand at the foot of the cliffs. Nothing in the data draws it: every vertex colour there is the neutral 0x80 (offscreen dump 2026-10-19), no generator or blended mesh adds it (hiding the `kin_fg` overlays changes only the cliff tops; drawing the blended decals after the solids changes nothing), and xi-model-viewer notes that FFXI has no cast-shadow pass. Lit per vertex, the cliff-base vertices (normals leaning away from the sun) are dark and their light spreads across the large sand triangles; lit per pixel, the summed light stayed above the clamp over nearly all of each triangle and only a thin dark line remained. Offscreen at the logged spot (display (-80.4, 1.9, -129.9), `dust`, 16:18-17:00) the per-vertex sand shows broad grey gradients at the cliff bases where per-pixel stayed white. Bibiki Bay's calibrated 15:58 sand is unchanged (166 vs 168 mean red); Southern San d'Oria's lamp pools come out slightly softer.
- Open: confirm the band in game against retail at a noted time and weather.

## Actor shadows (#7 rounds 1-2)

- Retail draws a character's silhouette on the ground along the light (Bibiki Bay captures, 2026-10-17; `image-11.png`) and, on the Config > Misc. "Shadows" Normal setting, a plain dark circle for other PCs, NPCs and monsters (`image-12.png`). The floor under a shadow keeps about half its brightness (33 vs 67 and 26 vs 46 in the two captures; `EntityRenderer.ShadowOpacity` 0.5, circles 0.45 at the centre, fading toward the rim, radius 0.22x the actor's height).
- `EntityRenderer.Shadow.cs`, `ActorShadowShaders` (D3D11 and Vulkan): each skinned actor's body is flattened onto the plane at its feet along the light it is lit by (`ShadowLightDirection`: the sun while it is up, the moon opposite it at night, an interior's own light), never lower than elevation 0.6 (`ShadowMinElevation`; a shadow is at most 1.33x the body height, as in retail's short 16:26 shadows in `image-6.png`), and pulled 0.4 yalm toward the camera (`ShadowEyePull`) so it stays over dunes and slopes.
- Settings: `ShadowMode` follows Config > Misc. "Shadows" each frame (`StockUiSettingKey.Shadows`; default Normal): High = every actor's silhouette, Normal = the local player's silhouette and circles for everyone else, Off = none. Actors with the server's ShadowFlag (`WorldEntity.HidesShadow`) or an event fade cast none.
- Round 2 fixes: round 1 blended the silhouette directly and wrote depth so overlapping parts darkened once; the coplanar depths flickered ("very flashy"), and ground overlays drawn later (Bibiki Bay's world-effect layers) covered it (F11, `image-14.png`). Now the shadows are drawn after the water and world-effect passes (`ZoneTerrainRenderer` calls `DrawQueuedShadows`), three times without writing depth: reset the colour target's alpha to 1 under the shadow, keep the smallest "light left" (1 - opacity) in that alpha, then multiply the colour by it and set the alpha back to 1. Each pixel darkens once, by the darkest shadow over it, with no depth fighting. (The depth buffer has no stencil, R32_Float, hence the alpha mask.)
- Not done: the soft edge of retail's silhouettes, shadows of static (unskinned) models (they get circles), and projection onto steep ground.

## Avatar lighting (#7 round 2)

- Each mesh piece carries a light multiplier in its 0x8010 render-properties block (+16, f32; layout from xi-model-viewer `ui/js/dat.js` `readRenderProps`, which reads it as `ambientMultiplier`). The avatars author 1.0-2.0 (Ifrit 1.4, Leviathan's fins 2.0, Garuda's feathers 1.6, Titan 0.6-1.3), ordinary monsters 1.0 (the demons 740 / 741 carry 2.0 on one piece). `EntityRenderer.ScaledActorLight` scales the actor's sun, moon and ambient light and its light cap by it per piece. Scaling only the ambient left Ifrit at under half retail's brightness.
- Models with a blur aura (0x5E: the avatars and Prime avatars) keep the zone's outdoor lights when they stand on a floor linked to an interior sub-environment. In retail's capture inside Dynamis-Jeuno's palace (`image-15.png`, `dark` weather) the avatars stay bright (Ifrit's body about (71, 37, 31)) while the goblins beside them darken; offscreen at the logged spot (display (12, -0.5, 43.1), 19:36) our avatars measured 32 (interior lights), 43 (interior lights x the multiplier) and 80 (outdoor lights x the multiplier). Estimated from that one capture: check outdoors and in other interiors.

## Dusk at Bibiki Bay's entrance cave (#6)

- The orange west-wall tint reported on 2026-09-25 came from the old xim colour conversion; with the byte/255 lights (2026-09-26) our dusk is pink-mauve like retail. Compared offscreen with the maintainer's captures `Bibiki_Lighting_Inside.png` (19:32, Windower (653.483, 899.612, -21.956)) and `Bibiki_Lighting_Outside.png` (19:50, Windower (644.170, 881.569, -20.065)), 2026-10-17. Mean colour of matching regions, retail vs ours:
  - The sky seen through the mouth reads r/g 1.34 in retail, 1.34 with our `suny` keyframes and 1.18 with `fine`, so the captures were most likely taken in `suny`.
  - Lit west-facing walls: retail (97, 72, 69), r/g 1.34, b/g 0.96; ours `fine` (129, 96, 89), r/g 1.34, b/g 0.93; ours `suny` (107, 69, 65), r/g 1.55.
  - Shaded walls and floor: retail b/g 1.29-1.31; ours `suny` 1.23-1.32, `fine` 1.16-1.19.
- So retail does light the west faces with a warm directional term after sunset (the lit wall is 1.8x the shaded one); there is no cut-off below the horizon to add. What remains is that in `suny` our lit faces are about 12% redder than retail. Ruled out: dropping the sun below the horizon (retail keeps the directional light), lighting terrain with the 0x2F model sun instead (it fits the dusk hue but would break the 15:58 calibration above). Not yet known: how the client interpolates the `suny` 18:00 -> 21:00 keyframes (sun (208, 130, 72) x1.5 -> black).


## Point lights

- *Chunk 2: Point lights (~15.6k generators).* The ZoneDef light table (header `+0x18`, 256 x `0x4C`, generator FourCC at `+0`) binds each `0x47` point-light generator to a slot by its own FourCC, and each placement's four 1-based references at `+0x54` are the only lights that shine on it (binding from xi-tools `docs/zone/format.md`). Light generators run through the emitter (`0x58` range / theta / multipliers `2^x`, clock theta `0x49`, progress `0x5B`-`0x5E`, parent `0x7E`/`0x7F`); life-0 generators (and 1-frame point lights) now emit once and live forever. Terrain shaders add `vColor x max(N.L, 0) x 2 x color x theta x (1 - d/range)^2` per referenced light inside the ambient + sun + moon clamp. Southern San d'Oria's lamps turn on at ~19:00 via their `pttm` theta curve, and the falloff/power were calibrated against Windower captures there (22:03 door lamps, 20:30 wall lamp).

## Indoor sub-environments and actor lighting

- *Indoor sub-environment lighting & point-light power (2026-09-25):* placements linked to a sub-environment (ZoneDef +0x4C, e.g. Metalworks' 627 `ev01` / 118 `ev02` groups) are lit by that environment's own 0x2F keyframes (per-environment scene uniform sets; indoor light direction decoded from the moon slot's signed bytes) instead of the outdoor weather, which had lit Metalworks' interiors with noon daylight. Point lights saturate per channel: colour = min(half-range colour x2 x power, 1) x 0.75, so power decides how pale a light is rather than scaling it (Metalworks' always-on power 12-40 orange lights wash to a pale yellow, San d'Oria's power-2 gold lamps stay gold); calibrated against Windower captures of both zones, where linear power made Metalworks 6-20x too bright and orange (the client's exact light pipeline is not decoded). Actors follow the same rule (2026-09-25): each entity probes the floor under its feet (`ZoneRaycaster.FindFloor`, re-cast after half a yalm of movement) and, on a floor linked to a sub-environment, takes that environment's lights, so characters in Bibiki Bay's entrance cave (`ev01`: sun/ambient ~0.27/0.31, blue-tinted) no longer glow with the outdoor noon light. Actors are lit with the 0x2F model lighting block (sun, moon, ambient) at twice the 0x80-neutral strength (`ActorLighting`, scale 2: character meshes carry no vertex colours): Windower captures in Bibiki Bay showed retail characters 2.6-3x brighter at night outdoors (22:46) and 2-2.5x in the entrance cave, matching the prediction; the summed actor light is capped at 0.75 (1.5x the texel, the pre-doubling noon peak) because at noon the doubled lights flattened skin tones to twice the texel; checked in-game across the F10 presets (day, dusk, night, overcast). Open: the cap is an estimate; compare characters at noon against a Windower capture of the same spot.
