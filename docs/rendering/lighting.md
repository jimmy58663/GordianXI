# Terrain, Actor & Point Lighting

> How Section 0x2F environment colours, point lights and sub-environments light terrain and actors. Calibrations were made against Windower captures of the legacy client. Status and open work: [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md) and GitHub Issues.

## Terrain lighting from 0x2F (chunk 1)

- **Chunk 1: Sand & Terrain Lighting Balance:** Calibrated diffuse and ambient lighting coefficients (`0.5 * amb + 0.5 * df0`) in terrain shaders to prevent overexposure of light-colored ground/sand while maintaining shadow depth. *Superseded (2026-09-24, legacy parity):* terrain/entity lighting is now `clamp(amb + sun + moon)` with 0x2F colors converted as the legacy client does (ambient `/510` + dark bias, clamp 0.5; sun/moon `* diffuseMult` + dark bias), and a moonlight term opposite the sun. *Superseded again (2026-09-26):* the 0x2F bytes now feed the lights as is, ambient = byte/255 and sun/moon = byte/255 x `light_power`, with no dark lift, no halving and no clamp before the lit vertex result (`ZoneEnvironmentSettings.AmbientToLight`/`DiffuseToLight`). Calibrated on a Windower capture of Bibiki Bay at 15:58 (Windower (642.7, 879.3, -20), `suny`): flat sunlit sand measured (182,105,77) in retail against (137,93,66) from the halved ambient; the byte/255 conversion renders it at (184,103,74) and brings the east-facing cliffs from (60,43,40) to (73,52,47) against retail's (76,50,47). Shadows were ~40% too dark before, which read as an over-strong sun. Particle daylight tints clamp at 1 (`StrongestLight`). Noon: see below (#7); retail clips there too, and GordianXI tones it down on purpose.

## Terrain highlights at noon (#7)

- **Retail clips to white.** The maintainer's capture `Retail Captures/Bright_Sand.png` (Valkurm Dunes, zone 103, about 13:17, clear, 2026-10-17) shows the flat sunlit sand saturated to pure white across the lower frame; only shaded dips and the cliffs keep texture. Our unclamped light gives the same: the summed light hits its clamp (1) and the sand texel is about 0.75, so 2 x light x texel is about 1.5 before the framebuffer clips it (measured offscreen at display (-150, ground, 0), 13:17, `fine`).
- **Deliberate exception to legacy parity.** The maintainer: "We don't have to match this, I would rather it be toned down to be more tolerable to look at." Terrain (opaque, cut-out, blended and water submeshes; not actors, effects or sky) now rolls off its highlights: a lit colour channel above `TerrainHighlightKnee` (0.65) keeps knee + e / (1 + e / (ceiling - knee)) of its excess e, approaching `TerrainHighlightCeiling` (0.9) instead of clipping (`ZoneShaders` `ToneTerrain`, uniform `ZoneSceneUniform.TerrainTone`). Valkurm's noon sand draws at about (215, 215, 212) instead of (255, 255, 255).
- **Why on the colour, not the summed light.** Bibiki Bay's calibrated 15:58 sand (above) already sits at the light clamp in red and green, so a shoulder on the light would have darkened it. On the output colour it only touches channels above 0.65: the 15:58 sand's red (0.72) moves by one level (168 to 167 mean over the sand), the 19:50 dusk cliffs (under 0.6) not at all.
- **Retail brightness (opt-in).** `ViewportSettings.RetailTerrainBrightness` (settings file; no Settings UI yet) restores the plain clamp: `ViewportRenderSettings.RetailTerrainBrightness` sets `ZoneTerrainRenderer.RetailTerrainBrightness` each frame, which zeroes `TerrainTone`.
- Open on #7: the actor light cap (0.75) and the sun glow behind `clod` / `suny` are still unchecked against retail.

## Per-vertex terrain lighting (#7 round 2, F10)

- Zone meshes are lit per vertex (Gouraud), as the legacy client's fixed-function pipeline does (`TerrainShaders`): the vertex shader sums ambient + sun + moon (opposite the sun) + the placement's point lights, times the vertex colour, clamps to 1 at the vertex, and the fragment shader interpolates that before the modulate-2x, the highlight shoulder and fog. Actors keep their own shaders.
- Why: the maintainer's capture outside Selbina (`image-13.png`, Valkurm Dunes, TESTING-2026-10-18 round 2) shows a soft grey band on the sand at the foot of the cliffs. Nothing in the data draws it: every vertex colour there is the neutral 0x80 (offscreen dump 2026-10-19), no generator or blended mesh adds it (hiding the `kin_fg` overlays changes only the cliff tops; drawing the blended decals after the solids changes nothing), and xi-model-viewer notes that FFXI has no cast-shadow pass. Lit per vertex, the cliff-base vertices (normals leaning away from the sun) are dark and their light spreads across the large sand triangles; lit per pixel, the summed light stayed above the clamp over nearly all of each triangle and only a thin dark line remained. Offscreen at the logged spot (display (-80.4, 1.9, -129.9), `dust`, 16:18-17:00) the per-vertex sand shows broad grey gradients at the cliff bases where per-pixel stayed white. Bibiki Bay's calibrated 15:58 sand is unchanged (166 vs 168 mean red); Southern San d'Oria's lamp pools come out slightly softer.
- Open: confirm the band in game against retail at a noted time and weather.

## Actor shadows (#7 rounds 1-2)

- Retail draws a character's silhouette on the ground along the light (Bibiki Bay captures, 2026-10-17; `image-11.png`) and, on the Config > Misc. "Shadows" Normal setting, a plain dark circle for other PCs, NPCs and monsters (`image-12.png`). The floor under a shadow keeps about half its brightness (33 vs 67 and 26 vs 46 in the two captures; `EntityRenderer.ShadowOpacity` 0.5, circles 0.45 at the centre, fading toward the rim, radius 0.22x the actor's height).
- `EntityRenderer.Shadow.cs`, `ActorShadowShaders` (D3D11 and Vulkan): each skinned actor's body is flattened onto the plane at its feet along the light it is lit by (`ShadowLightDirection`: the sun while it is up, the moon opposite it at night, an interior's own light), never lower than elevation 0.6 (`ShadowMinElevation`; a shadow is at most 1.33x the body height, as in retail's short 16:26 shadows in `image-6.png`), and pulled 0.4 yalm toward the camera (`ShadowEyePull`) so it stays over dunes and slopes.
- Settings: `ShadowMode` follows Config > Misc. "Shadows" each frame (`StockUiSettingKey.Shadows`; default Normal): High = every actor's silhouette, Normal = the local player's silhouette and circles for everyone else, Off = none. Actors with the server's ShadowFlag (`WorldEntity.HidesShadow`) or an event fade cast none.
- Round 2 fixes: round 1 blended the silhouette directly and wrote depth so overlapping parts darkened once; the coplanar depths flickered ("very flashy"), and ground overlays drawn later (Bibiki Bay's world-effect layers) covered it (F11, `image-14.png`). Now the shadows are drawn after the water and world-effect passes (`ZoneTerrainRenderer` calls `DrawQueuedShadows`), three times without writing depth: reset the colour target's alpha to 1 under the shadow, keep the smallest "light left" (1 - opacity) in that alpha, then multiply the colour by it and set the alpha back to 1. Each pixel darkens once, by the darkest shadow over it, with no depth fighting. (The depth buffer has no stencil, R32_Float, hence the alpha mask.)
- Not done: the soft edge of retail's silhouettes, shadows of static (unskinned) models (they get circles), and projection onto steep ground.

## Point lights (#428)

### What the data carries

- **Light table (ZoneDef, header `+0x18`).** 256 entries of `0x4C` bytes; each holds only the FourCC of a point-light generator at `+0`. The other `0x48` bytes are the client's runtime light-pool state: across every retail zone and interior DAT (298 zones, 238,592 entries, 2026-10-26) they are zero except the last six bytes of Phanauet Channel's (zone 1) last entry, FF where its table meets the next block. So the table has no colour, range, falloff or flags of its own. Binding from xi-tools (`docs/zone/format.md` "Light table"): the generator finds its slot by its own FourCC, and a placement's four 1-based references at record `+0x54` are the only lights that shine on it (at most four per object).
- **Interiors.** A building interior's DAT (sub-area, #68) has its own ZoneDef with its own light table (Lower Jeuno's Merry Minstrel's Meadhouse, sub-area 462: `l_01`-`l_03`). Its slots are moved past the zone's in-use slots (`ZoneTerrainRenderer.SubAreas`).
- **The light itself is the generator** (`0x05`, StandardSetup linked type `0x47`). Every property comes from its opcodes:
  - colour: `0x16` (RGBA bytes, alpha 0x80). Lower Jeuno's street lamps `pl*` (120, 80, 30); its arcade lights `c*` (99, 74, 42); the Meadhouse's (102-125, 68-85, 43-54); Castle Zvahl's torches (240, 150, 50).
  - range and power: `0x58` PointLightParams = range (yalms), theta (power), range multiplier and theta multiplier (stored as `x`, applied as `2^x`, or `1 + x` between -1 and 0). Ranges run 2.5-26, theta 2-40 (Meadhouse: range 3 / theta 4, 5 / 10, 10 / 10; Lower Jeuno `c*`: 8-18 / 20; Metalworks `c_1`: 26 / 40).
  - time of day: `0x49` (Clock.PLTheta) drives theta from a clock curve, e.g. `pttm` (the street lamps of Lower Jeuno and San d'Oria light at dusk); `0x5B`-`0x5E` drive range / theta by progress; `0x7E` / `0x7F` copy a parent's theta / range. Indoor lights carry none: always on.
  - weather: a light under a weather directory runs only in that weather.
- Nothing else exists: no falloff field, no flags, no per-light switch besides the clock curve. **Beyond xi-tools:** it says the rest of an entry is zero in the file; this is now checked over the whole install.

### How retail applies them

- **Rule: `vertex colour x max(N.L, 0) x colour x power / d^2`, nothing at or past the range**, added to the ambient + sun + moon sum before the clamp to 1 (`PointLightTableLayout`, one GLSL copy for every shader). Colour is the bytes / 128 (0x80-neutral, like the vertex colours); power is theta x theta multiplier x the particle's alpha multiplier; range is range x range multiplier. That is D3D's fixed-function point light with quadratic attenuation 1 / theta, which is why the data needs no falloff field. Strong lights saturate per channel near the lamp (pale yellow-white, the glowing lamp glass) and keep their hue further out.
- **Per vertex for zone meshes** (Gouraud, `TerrainShaders`, as with the sun: see the #7 round 2 section above); per pixel for actors and lit effects. At most four lights per placement, from its references.
- **A placement can opt out: record `+0x46` bit 0 set means no point lights**, although it still references some (`ZonePlacement.NoPointLights`; `ZoneDataLoader` drops its slots). *Inference* from retail captures, round 1 of 2026-10-27 (#428): retail lights the placements with the bit clear and leaves the ones with it set unlit.
- **Bit 1 set means point lights only in a building interior's DAT** (`ZonePlacement.InteriorLightsOnly`; `ResourceManager.TryLoadSubAreaInterior` passes `interior: true` to `ZoneDataLoader.ParseZoneContainer`). In a zone's own DAT such a placement takes none. *Inference* from round 3 of 2026-10-27 (#428 / #414, captured 2026-10-28): the Meadhouse's 48 placements and Lower Jeuno's `step01` carry the same four flag bytes (`00 00 02 01`), yet retail lights the first and leaves the second almost dark. The only lit placements with the byte at 2 are in an interior; every lit placement in a zone's own DAT has it at 0. Other zone placements with 2 that lose their lights: Lower Jeuno `step01` / `step01a`, Metalworks `to-guild01b` / `to-guild02b` / `underroom`, San d'Oria's guild-house pieces (13), and Castle Zvahl's torch stands `syoukudai01` and some wall pieces (210). Only `step01` is confirmed in retail.

  | Placement | `+0x46` | Retail |

  | Castle Zvahl `b_07b`, the torch-lit floor (#404) | 0 | brown torchlight |

Bit 0 is set on the arcade tunnels (`c` lights), Metalworks' halls (`c_`) and Lower Jeuno's shop goods and some walls (`pl` lamps). Bit 1 is the interior-only switch above. Lower Jeuno's light table also lists `c4` twice (1-based references 4 and 15). GordianXI binds its one generator to the first; which one retail uses is not known. **Beyond xi-tools:** it names `+0x44` four flag bytes with only the decal bit known.
- **Lit zone effects take their floor's environment** (`ZoneTerrainRenderer.EffectSceneUniform`): a lit generator over a cave's or interior's floor is lit by that sub-environment's sun and ambient, as the placements and actors there are, not by the outdoor sun. Metalworks' entrance overlays `ent_` / `ent1` (translucent copies of the floor and walls, vertex alpha 0x80 fading to 0, `SrcInvSrcAdd`, lit) took the noon sun and doubled the hall's floor and corridor walls (62 to 130); with `ev01`'s light they add almost nothing, as retail. The floor under the generator is an *inference*, following the actors' rule.

## Indoor sub-environments and actor lighting

- *Indoor sub-environment lighting & point-light power (2026-09-25):* placements linked to a sub-environment (ZoneDef +0x4C, e.g. Metalworks' 627 `ev01` / 118 `ev02` groups) are lit by that environment's own 0x2F keyframes (per-environment scene uniform sets; indoor light direction decoded from the moon slot's signed bytes) instead of the outdoor weather, which had lit Metalworks' interiors with noon daylight. *Superseded (#428):* point lights used to saturate per channel (colour = min(colour x 2 x power, 1) x 0.75); they now follow the fixed-function rule in [Point lights](#point-lights-428). Actors follow the same rule (2026-09-25): each entity probes the floor under its feet (`ZoneRaycaster.FindFloor`, re-cast after half a yalm of movement) and, on a floor linked to a sub-environment, takes that environment's lights, so characters in Bibiki Bay's entrance cave (`ev01`: sun/ambient ~0.27/0.31, blue-tinted) no longer glow with the outdoor noon light. Actors are lit with the 0x2F model lighting block (sun, moon, ambient) at twice the 0x80-neutral strength (`ActorLighting`, scale 2: character meshes carry no vertex colours): Windower captures in Bibiki Bay showed retail characters 2.6-3x brighter at night outdoors (22:46) and 2-2.5x in the entrance cave, matching the prediction; the summed actor light is capped at 0.75 (1.5x the texel, the pre-doubling noon peak) because at noon the doubled lights flattened skin tones to twice the texel; checked in-game across the F10 presets (day, dusk, night, overcast). Open: the cap is an estimate; compare characters at noon against a Windower capture of the same spot.

## Ship-zone sea surfaces (#430)

The Manaclipper's open sea (`umim` / `umin`) is a lit generator layer, so its brightness depends on the zone's 0x2F sun and ambient as particle lighting (`clamp(colour x ambient + colour x N.L x sun)`, then modulate-2x). By day zone 3's lights (sun 1.14, ambient 0.65 at 14:19) lift the sea about 1.4x over unlit, and retail's sea is darker than even our unlit one. Retail's sea also darkens about 4x under clouds ((11, 17, 20) against (45, 54, 53)), where zone 3's clouds environment (sun 1.0, ambient 0.55) dims ours by about 13 %; see [Ship zones' water](particles.md#ship-zones-water-430-424). Not changed: the lighting held for #414 / #404 / #428 is not touched for this.
